Travel Tips

Time To Visit Thailand

The ideal time to visit Thailand is between November and early April, when the weather is cool and dry across most regions; this period also avoids the heavy monsoon rains and sees fewer crowds than the peak holiday weeks.

Plan Thailand

For a 1‑week trip, concentrate on Bangkok’s highlights—Grand Palace, Wat Pho, and a day trip to Ayutthaya—then finish with a quick overnight stay on Koh Samet for a taste of beach relaxation.

Plan Thailand

A 2‑week itinerary works well by splitting time between the north and south: spend 4‑5 days exploring Chiang Mai’s temples, night markets, and nearby Doi Inthanon, then fly to the Andaman coast for a week of island hopping on Phuket, Krabi, and the Phi Phi islands.

Plan Thailand

With 3 weeks you can experience Thailand’s full spectrum: start in Bangkok (3 days), head north to Chiang Mai and Pai (5‑6 days for culture and jungle trekking), return south for the Gulf islands (Koh Samui, Koh Tao, Koh Phangan – 7 days), and wrap up with a relaxed stay in the historic city of Ayutthaya or the coastal town of Hua Hin (3‑4 days).

Weather Info Thailand

Thailand has three main seasons: hot (Mar‑May) with temps 30‑40 °C, rainy (Jun‑Oct) bringing heavy monsoon showers especially in the north and east, and cool (Nov‑Feb) with milder 24‑30 °C and lower humidity; travelers should pack light, breathable clothing, a rain jacket for the monsoon months, and stay alert for occasional flooding or tropical storms.

Budget Tips Thailand

Budget travelers can get by on about ฿800‑฿1,200 ($22‑$33) per day by staying in hostels, eating street food, and using local transport, while mid‑range visitors typically spend ฿2,000‑฿3,500 ($55‑$95) and luxury travelers often exceed ฿6,000 ($190).

Budget Tips Thailand

Save money by buying a prepaid tourist SIM for data, using the reliable yet cheap songthaew or motorbike taxis, and purchasing tickets for attractions online in advance to snag discounts.

Transport Thailand

Use the BTS Skytrain and MRT subway in Bangkok for fast, cheap, air‑conditioned travel; get a Rabbit Card or stored‑value MRT card to avoid queues.

Transport Thailand

For intercity trips, book a seat on the State Railway’s “Special Express” trains or use budget airlines like AirAsia and Nok Air; consider night trains with sleeper berths to save on both transport and a night’s accommodation.

Special Events Thailand

Songkran (Thai New Year) in mid‑April turns streets into massive water fights, so book lodging early, wear waterproof shoes, and be ready to join the fun while respecting local customs.

Special Events Thailand

Loy Krathong in November (and the Yi Peng lantern sky in Chiang Mai) offers magical river lantern releases; bring a waterproof bag for electronics and arrive early for the best viewing spots.

Special Events Thailand

Chinese New Year, usually in January or February, lights up Bangkok’s Chinatown with dragon dances and street food stalls—note that banks and some offices close, so plan cash withdrawals ahead.

Special Events Thailand

The Phuket Vegetarian Festival in October features nine days of strict vegetarian fasting, fire‑walking processions, and vibrant parades; respect participants’ observances and try the special vegetarian dishes.

Cultural Norm Thailand

Always show respect for the Thai monarchy; avoid any criticism, stand for the national anthem, and treat images of the king with reverence.

Cultural Norm Thailand

When visiting temples, dress modestly by covering shoulders and knees, remove your shoes before entering, and keep your head low as a sign of humility.

Cultural Norm Thailand

Use the traditional wai greeting with palms together and a slight bow, and remember that pointing your feet at people or religious objects is considered rude.

Safety Thailand

Carry bottled water, drink only sealed beverages, get hepatitis A, typhoid and routine vaccines before you go, and be cautious with street food hygiene; also avoid poorly lit or isolated neighborhoods after dark and always watch traffic, as road safety can be unpredictable.

Food Drink Thailand

Don’t miss Thailand’s iconic dishes—Pad Thai, Tom Yum Goong, Som Tum, and mango sticky rice—best enjoyed from bustling street stalls or night‑market vendors where the flavors are freshest.

Food Drink Thailand

Explore regional specialties: the creamy coconut‑curry noodle soup Khao Soi in the north, spicy grilled chicken (Gai Yang) with sticky rice and papaya salad in Isan, and the southern seafood curries that showcase abundant fresh fish and lemongrass.

Food Drink Thailand

If you have dietary restrictions, remember many dishes contain fish sauce, shrimp paste, or peanuts; politely ask for "mai sai nam pla" (no fish sauce) or "jay" (vegetarian) and specify your spice tolerance to ensure a comfortable meal.
